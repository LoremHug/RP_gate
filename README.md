## The four audits

Every claim entering the RP gate is assessed through four questions:

**R1 — Object reification.**  
Does the claim introduce an entity where there is only a process?  
*Object boundaries not derived from the manifold are hidden postulates.*

**R2 — External evaluation frame.**  
Does the claim invoke a criterion that stands outside the manifold?  
*External frames are coordinate choices dressed as ground truth.*

**R3 — Scale injection.**  
Does the claim introduce a numerical scale without deriving it from the topology?  
*Unexplained scales are undischarged Z_hidden.*

**R4 — Agency attribution.**  
Does the claim attribute volition or goal-directedness to a process?  
*Agency is a perceptual-interface description, not a manifold property.*

A claim that **passes all four** is a candidate for structural verification.  
A claim that **fails any one** is not merely weak — it is structurally malformed.

The gate produces two outcomes only:
- **Dissolution** — the claim had no structural ground; the question is removed, not answered.
- **Upgrade** — the claim had a structural ground not yet made explicit; epistemic status rises.

There is no third outcome.

## What the RP gate is not

- It is **not a validator**. It does not confirm frameworks. It aligns them to their structural remainder.
- It is **not a falsification criterion** in the Popperian sense. The manifold is the precondition of any falsification — it cannot be falsified from within a coordinate expression of it.
- It is **not subjective**. Each of R1–R4 maps to a precise structural diagnostic. The gate fires or it does not.

The correct test of any theoretical framework is not *"does it reproduce the observed numbers?"* (necessary but not sufficient) but *"what survives the Reality Protocol?"*

Whatever survives is the physics. Whatever dissolves was a coordinate choice.
